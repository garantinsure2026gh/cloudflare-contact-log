Keeping a Clear Log of Calls and Emails While an SR-22 Is Active

While an SR-22 requirement is active, you will probably speak with two kinds of offices: the insurer that holds the policy and the state agency connected to the requirement. A contact log holds the details that matter later and fade quickly. It is one page, in date order, recording every call and email in the same shape. It does not say what any office requires or how long anything takes, because those answers vary by state and by insurer and belong with the offices themselves.

The log sits alongside the rest of your SR-22 paperwork records and points into them. A call is a line in the log. The document it produced is a file in the folder. The two are connected by a date and a reference number.

What every entry records

Use the same fields in the same order for each contact, so the page can be read quickly.

  • Date and time. Written before the call or email.
  • Channel. Phone, email, online message, or in person.
  • Office and contact. The office as it names itself, and the name of the person if given.
  • Reference numbers. Any case, policy, filing, or ticket number that was used or given. Copy them exactly.
  • Questions asked. One line each, as you asked them.
  • Answers received. Under each question, in the speaker's words where you can manage it. Mark anything you are unsure you heard correctly.
  • Documents sent or received. The name of each, its date, and where your copy is stored.
  • Follow-up items. What happens next, who owes it, and the date it is expected. Written as a verb: send, call, wait for.

Leave a field blank rather than guessing. An empty field is a reminder to ask.

After a phone call

Write the entry before doing anything else. Then send a short message to the same office restating what you understood and asking them to confirm or correct it. Note in the log that you sent it and where the copy is. When a reply arrives, add a line for it and note whether it confirms or corrects what you wrote.

After an email

Copy the subject line and the date into the log, and record which folder or account holds the message. If the email contains a request or a deadline, that becomes a follow-up item with its date. If it raises a question, write it under the entry.

Keeping the log with the paperwork

Documents mentioned in the log should be stored where it says they are, under a naming rule that starts with the date. The short checklist on what to keep in a renewal folder describes the set of documents most likely to come up in these conversations.

Follow-up items with dates also belong on a calendar. The routine in a renewal calendar for the active months explains how to schedule a short check a couple of weeks after each contact so a promised reply does not quietly go missing.

Reviewing it

Once a week, read the follow-up items. Mark any that were completed, with the date and where the proof is. Update any whose date has passed without a result and decide whether to ask again. Add the review date at the top of the page.

For the wider set of habits around this, there is a companion guide to SR-22 paperwork routines that covers the folders and lists the log connects to. On its own, the log answers the question that comes up most: who said what, and when.
